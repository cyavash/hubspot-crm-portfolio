CONNECTED DATA / ORGANIZATION DESIGN

A clearer structure starts with connected data

Organization design is easier to discuss when people can see the same information, understand who owns it and explore changes together.

Article layout example · Written for this portfolio by Siavash Zahir

ON THIS PAGE

Start with the current organization Make ownership explicit Explore a scenario Keep the picture useful

Start with the current organization

An organization chart is most useful when it reflects the work people actually do. Bring roles, teams and reporting relationships into one view before proposing a new structure.

Review gaps with the people closest to the work. A shared baseline makes the next conversation more specific: what should change, who is affected, and what information is still missing?

Make ownership explicit

Good data needs a clear owner. Decide who maintains role information, approves reporting changes and checks the quality of imported records.

A useful structure connects people to responsibilities, not just boxes to lines.

Agree on a small set of rules before connecting systems. Consistent naming and review steps reduce ambiguity when the organization changes.

Explore a scenario before committing

Use the baseline to compare an alternative. Keep the business question narrow: how would a proposed team structure change accountability or collaboration?

  1. Define the decision and the constraints.
  2. Compare the proposed structure with the current one.
  3. Record the assumptions that need review.

Keep the picture useful

Organization design is an ongoing practice. Set a regular review cadence and make updates part of the operating process, so the chart remains a tool for decisions rather than a snapshot that quickly loses relevance.
